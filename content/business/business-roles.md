Whoever owns the website and the ad accounts decides how customer data is used. For most businesses that's the business itself, which makes it the **controller**. Everyone else in the flow either works on your instructions or runs under your own agreements with them.

## Roles in this flow

| Party | Role in this flow | Role | Paperwork |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Your business | Owns the website, data.yourbusiness.com and the ad accounts. Decides why data is collected. | Controller | Privacy notice; record of processing; your contracts below |
| Your agency or marketing partner | May run campaigns or have access to the ad accounts | Usually processor; joint controller if it sets its own purposes | Art. 28 DPA, or Art. 26 arrangement |
| TDS | Sets up, tests and maintains your first-party infrastructure through the access you grant | Processor | [Data processing agreement](page:data-processing-agreement) |
| Lupe dashboard | Reads aggregated, non-personal statistics and configuration to alert you to problems | Does not process your customers' personal data | [Terms of service](page:terms-of-service) |
| Stape | Hosts your server-side container on data.yourbusiness.com | Your processor, in an account you hold | Stape's terms, accepted by you |
| Consent platform (e.g. CookieYes) | Logs consent choices and stores consent records | Your processor, in an account you hold | Provider's DPA |
| Shopify, Google, Meta, TikTok | Your store, analytics and ad platforms | As set out in each platform's own data terms | Platform business terms, accepted in your accounts |

:::tip Good to know
The ad platforms' role is the same whether data comes from a browser pixel or from your server. A first-party setup doesn't create a new relationship with any platform. It gives you control over what reaches them.
:::

Roles are described in UK and EU terms. Under US state laws, your business is the "business" or "controller", and TDS, Stape and your CMP are "service providers" or "processors".
